Every "best tools" list reads the same: fifty logos, no context, no
sequencing. Here's the version I wish someone had handed me instead — five
things, in the order I actually needed them, with the honest reason each one
mattered.

## 1. A single place for money in and money out

Before revenue, before a company name, before anything — one spreadsheet or
tool tracking what came in and what went out. Not for taxes. For truth. It's
the fastest way to know if you're building a business or funding a hobby.

## 2. A calendar you actually trust

I spent my first year running two calendars in my head and one on paper. The
moment I moved everything — client calls, deep work blocks, even "do nothing"
time — into one tool, decisions got easier because I could finally *see* my
constraints instead of guessing at them.

## 3. Something to capture ideas before they evaporate

Founders lose more good ideas to "I'll remember this" than to bad judgment.
Whatever you use — notes app, voice memos, a physical notebook — the only
requirement is that it's faster to open than your resistance to writing
things down.

## 4. A lightweight project tracker, used honestly

Not project management theater. Just a list of what's actually in motion,
updated weekly, that you're willing to look at when things are going badly —
not just when they're going well.

## 5. A place to write in public

This one came last for me, and I regret that. Writing down what you're
learning, even badly, even to an audience of twelve people, forces a level of
clarity that thinking alone never does.

None of these are glamorous. That's sort of the point — the tools that
mattered were the ones I actually used every day, not the ones that looked
best in a screenshot.
