For a long time I told myself the job was fine. Good pay, good people, a title
that meant something at dinner parties. The problem was never the job — it was
that I'd quietly redefined "responsible" to mean "predictable," and I'd stopped
noticing the difference.

## The math I kept doing wrong

Every time I ran the numbers on leaving, I ran them the same way: current
salary, minus zero, forever. I never once ran them the other way — current
salary, minus zero, **for how long, exactly?** A year of savings is not
forever. It's a runway. And a runway is something you can plan around instead
of being paralyzed by.

> You don't need a guarantee to start. You need a number you can survive
> missing.

## What actually changed my mind

It wasn't a single dramatic moment — no ultimatum, no blow-up meeting. It was
three ordinary realizations stacked on top of each other over about six
months:

1. The skills I was building at my job had a shelf life I couldn't see from
   the inside.
2. The people I admired most hadn't waited for permission — they'd taken a
   smaller bet earlier than felt comfortable.
3. "Not yet" had quietly become my permanent answer, not a temporary one.

## If you're where I was

You don't need to quit tomorrow. You need to stop treating your current
situation as the default and everything else as the risk. Comfortable isn't
neutral — it's a decision you're making every single day you stay.
